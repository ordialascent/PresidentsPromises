## ¶1
1. Mr. Chairman, Mr. Vice President, delegates to this convention, and fellow citizens:

## ¶2
1. In 75 days, I hope we enjoy a victory that is the size of the heart of Texas.
2. Nancy and I extend our deep thanks to the Lone Star State and the "Big D"—the city of Dallas—for all their warmth and hospitality.

## ¶3
1. Four years ago I didn't know precisely every duty of this office, and not too long ago, I learned about some new ones from the first graders of Corpus Christi School in Chambersburg, Pennsylvania.
2. Little Leah Kline was asked by her teacher to describe my duties.
3. She said: "The President goes to meetings.
4. He helps the animals.
5. The President gets frustrated.
6. He talks to other Presidents."
7. How does wisdom begin at such an early age?

## ¶4
1. Tonight, with a full heart and deep gratitude for your trust, I accept your nomination for the Presidency of the United States.
2. I will campaign on behalf of the principles of our party which lift America confidently into the future.

## ¶5
1. America is presented with the clearest political choice of half a century.
2. The distinction between our two parties and the different philosophy of our political opponents are at the heart of this campaign and America's future.

## ¶6
1. I've been campaigning long enough to know that a political party and its leadership can't change their colors in 4 days.
2. We won't, and no matter how hard they tried, our opponents didn't in San Francisco.
3. We didn't discover our values in a poll taken a week before the convention.
4. And we didn't set a weathervane on top of the Golden Gate Bridge before we started talking about the American family.

## ¶7
1. The choices this year are not just between two different personalities or between two political parties.
2. They're between two different visions of the future, two fundamentally different ways of governing-their government of pessimism, fear, and limits, or ours of hope, confidence, and growth.

## ¶8
1. Their government sees people only as members of groups; ours serves all the people of America as individuals.
2. Theirs lives in the past, seeking to apply the old and failed policies to an era that has passed them by.
3. Ours learns from the past and strives to change by boldly charting a new course for the future.
4. Theirs lives by promises, the bigger, the better.
5. We offer proven, workable answers.

## ¶9
1. Our opponents began this campaign hoping that America has a poor memory.
2. Well, let's take them on a little stroll down memory lane.
3. Let's remind them of how a 4.8-percent inflation rate in 1976 became back-to-back years of double-digit inflation—the worst since World War I—punishing the poor and the elderly, young couples striving to start their new lives, and working people struggling to make ends meet.

## ¶10
1. Inflation was not some plague borne on the wind; it was a deliberate part of their official economic policy, needed, they said, to maintain prosperity.
2. They didn't tell us that with it would come the highest interest rates since the Civil War.
3. As average monthly mortgage payments more than doubled, home building nearly ground to a halt; tens of thousands of carpenters and others were thrown out of work.
4. And who controlled both Houses of the Congress and the executive branch at that time?
5. Not us, not us.

## ¶11
1. Campaigning across America in 1980, we saw evidence everywhere of industrial decline.
2. And in rural America, farmers' costs were driven up by inflation.
3. They were devastated by a wrongheaded grain embargo and were forced to borrow money at exorbitant interest rates just to get by.
4. And many of them didn't get by.
5. Farmers have to fight insects, weather, and the marketplace; they shouldn't have to fight their own government.

## ¶12
1. The high interest rates of 1980 were not talked about in San Francisco.
2. But how about taxes?
3. They were talked about in San Francisco.
4. Will Rogers once said he never met a man he didn't like.
5. Well, if I could paraphrase Will, our friends in the other party have never met a tax they didn't like or hike.

## ¶13
1. Under their policies, tax rates have gone up three times as much for families with children as they have for everyone else over these past three decades.
2. In just the 5 years before we came into office, taxes roughly doubled.

## ¶14
1. Some who spoke so loudly in San Francisco of fairness were among those who brought about the biggest single, individual tax increase in our history in 1977, calling for a series of increases in the Social Security payroll tax and in the amount of pay subject to that tax.
2. The bill they passed called for two additional increases between now and 1990, increases that bear down hardest on those at the lower income levels.
3. The Census Bureau confirms that, because of the tax laws we inherited, the number of households at or below the poverty level paying Federal income tax more than doubled between 1980 and 1982.
4. Well, they received some relief in 1983, when our across-the-board tax cut was fully in place.
5. And they'll get more help when indexing goes into effect this January.

## ¶15
1. Our opponents have repeatedly advocated eliminating indexing.
2. Would that really hurt the rich?
3. No, because the rich are already in the top brackets.
4. But those working men and women who depend on a cost-of-living adjustment just to keep abreast of inflation would find themselves pushed into higher tax brackets and wouldn't even be able to keep even with inflation because they'd be paying a higher income tax.
5. That's bracket creep; and our opponents are for it, and we're against it.

## ¶16
1. It's up to us to see that all our fellow citizens understand that confiscatory taxes, costly social experiments, and economic tinkering were not just the policies of a single administration.
2. For the 26 years prior to January of 1981, the opposition party controlled both Houses of Congress.
3. Every spending bill and every tax for more than a quarter of a century has been of their doing.

## ¶17
1. About a decade ago, they said Federal spending was out of control, so they passed a budget control act and, in the next 5 years, ran up deficits of $260 billion.
2. Some control.

## ¶18
1. In 1981 we gained control of the Senate and the executive branch.
2. With the help of some concerned Democrats in the House we started a policy of tightening the Federal budget instead of the family budget.

## ¶19
1. A task force chaired by Vice President George Bush—the finest Vice President this country has ever had—it eliminated unnecessary regulations that had been strangling business and industry.

## ¶20
1. And while we have our friends down memory lane, maybe they'd like to recall a gimmick they designed for their 1976 campaign.
2. As President Ford told us the night before last, adding the unemployment and inflation rates, they got what they called a misery index.
3. In '76 it came to 12 1/2 percent.
4. They declared the incumbent had no right to seek reelection with that kind of a misery index.
5. Well, 4 years ago, in the 1980 election, they didn't mention the misery index, possibly because it was then over 20 percent.
6. And do you know something?
7. They won't mention it in this election either.
8. It's down to 11.6 and dropping.

## ¶21
1. By nearly every measure, the position of poor Americans worsened under the leadership of our opponents.
2. Teenage drug use, out-of-wedlock births, and crime increased dramatically.
3. Urban neighborhoods and schools deteriorated.
4. Those whom government intended to help discovered a cycle of dependency that could not be broken.
5. Government became a drug, providing temporary relief, but addiction as well.

## ¶22
1. And let's get some facts on the table that our opponents don't want to hear.
2. The biggest annual increase in poverty took place between 1978 and 1981—over 9 percent each year, in the first 2 years of our administration.
3. Well, I should—pardon me—I didn't put a period in there.
4. In the first 2 years of our administration, that annual increase fell to 5.3 percent.
5. And 1983 was the first year since 1978 that there was no appreciable increase in poverty at all.

## ¶23
1. Pouring hundreds of billions of dollars into programs in order to make people worse off was irrational and unfair.
2. It was time we ended this reliance on the government process and renewed our faith in the human process.

## ¶24
1. In 1980 the people decided with us that the economic crisis was not caused by the fact that they lived too well.
2. Government lived too well.
3. It was time for tax increases to be an act of last resort, not of first resort.

## ¶25
1. The people told the liberal leadership in Washington, "Try shrinking the size of government before you shrink the size of our paychecks."

## ¶26
1. Our government was also in serious trouble abroad.
2. We had aircraft that couldn't fly and ships that couldn't leave port.
3. Many of our military were on food stamps because of meager earnings, and reenlistments were down.
4. Ammunition was low, and spare parts were in short supply.

## ¶27
1. Many of our allies mistrusted us.
2. In the 4 years before we took office, country after country fell under the Soviet yoke.
3. Since January 20th, 1981, not 1 inch of soil has fallen to the Communists.

## ¶31
1. But worst of all, Americans were losing the confidence and optimism about the future that has made us unique in the world.
2. Parents were beginning to doubt that their children would have the better life that has been the dream of every American generation.

## ¶32
1. We can all be proud that pessimism is ended.
2. America is coming back and is more confident than ever about the future.
3. Tonight, we thank the citizens of the United States whose faith and unwillingness to give up on themselves or this country saved us all.

## ¶33
1. Together, we began the task of controlling the size and activities of the government by reducing the growth of its spending while passing a tax program to provide incentives to increase productivity for both workers and industry.
2. Today, a working family earning $25,000 has about $2,900 more in purchasing power than if tax and inflation rates were still at the 1980 level.

## ¶34
1. Today, of all the major industrial nations of the world, America has the strongest economic growth; one of the lowest inflation rates; the fastest rate of job creations—6 1/2 million jobs in the last year and a half—a record 600,000 business incorporations in 1983; and the largest increase in real, after-tax personal income since World War II.
2. We're enjoying the highest level of business investment in history, and America has renewed its leadership in developing the vast new opportunities in science and high technology.
3. America is on the move again and expanding toward new eras of opportunity for everyone.

## ¶35
1. Now, we're accused of having a secret.
2. Well, if we have, it is that we're going to keep the mighty engine of this nation revved up.
3. And that means a future of sustained economic growth without inflation that's going to create for our children and grandchildren a prosperity that finally will last.

## ¶36
1. Today our troops have newer and better equipment; their morale is higher.
2. The better armed they are, the less likely it is they will have to use that equipment.
3. But if, heaven forbid, they're ever called upon to defend this nation, nothing would be more immoral than asking them to do so with weapons inferior to those of any possible opponent.

## ¶37
1. We have also begun to repair our valuable alliances, especially our historic NATO alliance.
2. Extensive discussions in Asia have enabled us to start a new round of diplomatic progress there.

## ¶38
1. In the Middle East, it remains difficult to bring an end to historic conflicts, but we're not discouraged.
2. And we shall always maintain our pledge never to sell out one of our closest friends, the State of Israel.

## ¶39
1. Closer to home, there remains a struggle for survival for free Latin American States, allies of ours.
2. They valiantly struggle to prevent Communist takeovers fueled massively by the Soviet Union and Cuba.
3. Our policy is simple: We are not going to betray our friends, reward the enemies of freedom, or permit fear and retreat to become American policies—especially in this hemisphere.

## ¶40
1. None of the four wars in my lifetime came about because we were too strong.
2. It's weakness that invites adventurous adversaries to make mistaken judgments.
3. America is the most peaceful, least warlike nation in modern history.
4. We are not the cause of all the ills of the world.
5. We're a patient and generous people.
6. But for the sake of our freedom and that of others, we cannot permit our reserve to be confused with a lack of resolve.

## ¶107
1. All right.
2. In 1980 we asked the people of America, "Are you better off than you were 4 years ago?"
3. Well, the people answered then by choosing us to bring about a change.
4. We have every reason now, 4 years later, to ask that same question again, for we have made a change.

## ¶108
1. The American people joined us and helped us.
2. Let us ask for their help again to renew the mandate of 1980, to move us further forward on the road we presently travel, the road of common sense, of people in control of their own destiny; the road leading to prosperity and economic expansion in a world at peace.

## ¶109
1. As we ask for their help, we should also answer the central question of public service: Why are we here?
2. What do we believe in?
3. Well for one thing, we're here to see that government continues to serve the people and not the other way around.
4. Yes, government should do all that is necessary, but only that which is necessary.

## ¶110
1. We don't lump people by groups or special interests.

## ¶111
1. We believe in the uniqueness of each individual.
2. We believe in the sacredness of human life.
3. For some time now we've all fallen into a pattern of describing our choice as left or right.
4. It's become standard rhetoric in discussions of political philosophy.
5. But is that really an accurate description of the choice before us?

## ¶112

2. Stalin.
3. Hitler.

## ¶113
1. Isn't our choice really not one of left or right, but of up or down?

3. The alternative is the dream conceived by our Founding Fathers, up to the ultimate in individual freedom consistent with an orderly society.
